Tool 2, Inquiry 1–3 & the IA

L1 Tools Skills Handbook · Modules 2, 4 & 5 · IB Chemistry (Guide 2025, p.34–36 + IA section) · Student version

Part A · Tool 2: Technology

A1 · Sensors and data loggers

Sensors turn a physical variable into a stream of digital data: pH probes (calibrated with buffer solutions before use), temperature probes, conductivity and colorimeter links to data-logging software. Compared with reading by eye, they give continuous data (a full cooling curve, not six points), remove reading/rounding errors, and reveal shape detail — exactly what you need to determine rates from gradients (U6) or follow a titration curve (U8).

A2 · Databases and simulations

The guide expects you to identify and extract data from databases and to generate data from models and simulations. For the IA: if you use a database or a simulation, you must show how the data was sampled, filtered and extracted — with screenshots of the method (this is a TSM requirement). And remember what you already know about models from NOS: every simulation is a model with assumptions; a simulation result is evidence about the model, not automatically about the world.

A3 · Spreadsheets for processing and graphing

Spreadsheets are the bridge from raw data to a processed table and a graph: formulas for derived quantities, automatic plotting, best-fit lines and R2 (Tool 3's fit judgment). In P1B you do this by hand on paper — in the IA you are expected to do it properly with software.

Part B · The inquiry process (Inquiry 1–3)

B1 · Inquiry 1: Exploring and designing

Research questions worth investigating are not answerable by a search engine, are not a re-run of a practical you already did, and are not self-evident from the syllabus. A scoring RQ is specific (names the system and the quantity), contextualized (why it matters / real situation), and measurable (points to data): "How does the concentration of ethanoic acid in brand X vinegar, determined by titration with standard NaOH, compare with the label value of 5%?" beats "What is the acidity of vinegar?" by every descriptor band.

Variables: independent (what you set), dependent (what you measure, with its instrument and uncertainty — Tool 3), controlled (what you must hold constant and how). Methodology: enough repeats to show reproducibility, a sensible range and interval of the independent variable, and enough detail that another student could reproduce it — that last phrase is the literal wording of the IA Research design criterion.

B2 · Inquiry 2: Collecting and processing data

Raw data tables with units and uncertainties in every column header; instrument uncertainty stated once per column; means of repeats (uncertainty unchanged — Tool 3); processed quantities to appropriate significant figures; graphs with best-fit and uncertainty bars where the data justify them. "Sufficient data" means enough points/repeats to support the conclusion you intend to draw — not a fixed number.

B3 · Inquiry 3: Concluding and evaluating

The conclusion compares your processed result with a literature or theoretical value, through the uncertainty: if the ranges overlap, the data are consistent with the theory; if not, there is something to explain. The evaluation then names systematic errors in the method (with their direction — does each make the result too high or too low?) and random scatter (reduced by repeats), and proposes targeted improvements — each improvement must address a named limitation, never "do it more carefully".

Part C · The IA: rules of the game

FactDetail
NatureOne individual scientific investigation, woven into normal teaching — not a bolt-on after the course
Time10 hours of practical work (planning + data collection + processing + report)
Criteria (SL = HL)Research design · Data analysis · Conclusion · Evaluation — 6 marks each, 25% each, 24 total
GroupsUp to 3 students may share an investigation, but every student's data and report must be their own
MarkingRead the whole report before awarding each criterion; evidence for one criterion can sit anywhere in the report

How this handbook feeds the IA: Tool 1 (reproducible methodology, apparatus precision) and the Research design criterion; Tool 2 + Tool 3 and Data analysis (tables, uncertainties, graphs, best fit); Inquiry 3 and Conclusion + Evaluation (compare through uncertainty, name error types, targeted improvements). Every IA mark is trainable before the IA starts — that is the whole point of the L1 → L2 → L3 line of this course.

Practice set

Q1. Improve this research question into one that would score in the top band of Research design: "Does temperature affect solubility?"

 

Q2. For your improved RQ: state the independent, dependent and two controlled variables, naming the apparatus for the dependent variable.

 

Q3. Critique: a student measured the solubility at five temperatures, once each, and wrote "the results were slightly wrong because of human error". Identify three specific weaknesses and one improvement for each.

 

Q4. Match each report element to its criterion: (a) uncertainty bars on the graph; (b) "another student could repeat this from my method"; (c) "the literature value 5.0 ± 0.2% lies within my range 4.7–5.1%"; (d) "the heat lost through the lid made my value too low; use a deeper calorimeter".

 

Q5. Exit ticket: your IA result is 4.6% where the label says 5.0% and your percentage uncertainty is 1%. State, in two sentences, what a top-band evaluation would do with that fact.
